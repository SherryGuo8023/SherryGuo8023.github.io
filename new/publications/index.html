Papers

Research from building Bside, at the meeting point of games and HCI.

  • Bounded Autonomy: Controlling LLM Characters in Live Multiplayer Games

    Yunjia Guo, Jinghan Zhu, Siyu Wang, Haixin Qiao

    arXiv:2604.04703 [cs.HC]. Preprint, 2026.

    How should LLM characters take part in live multiplayer play while staying executable in the shared world, coherent with the other characters, and steerable by players? The paper organises character control around three interfaces: agent-to-agent communication kept stable with probabilistic reply-chain decay, world actions grounded through embeddings with fallbacks, and a "whisper" that lets players nudge a character without taking it over. Deployed in Bside; a formative analysis of the live game shows the architecture keeping interaction stable and player interventions landing.

  • Dobit to Bside: Grounding LLM Characters into Executable Gameplay

    Yunjia Guo, Jinghan Zhu

    AIIDE 2026, Case Studies track. Accepted, to appear, 2026.

    A case study of the road from Dobit, a single AI companion, to Bside, a shipped multiplayer social simulation: what it took to turn open-ended model output into actions the game can actually execute, and what we learned across three generations of character systems. Presentation scheduled for November 2026; the paper will be linked once published.

  • Raise, Don't Replace: Two Kinds of Connection in AI Character Products

    Jinghan Zhu, Yunjia Guo

    BritCHI 2026 (39th International BCS Human-Computer Interaction Conference), Short Papers track. Accepted, to appear, 2026.

    A short paper that separates two kinds of connection an AI character product can support, connection with the character and connection between people that the character catalyses, and argues for designs that raise human relationships rather than replace them. Brunel University of London, 1 to 3 November 2026.
