Papers

What happens when you give game characters a language model and let go of the script.

  • Bounded Autonomy: Controlling LLM Characters in Live Multiplayer Games

    First author. arXiv:2604.04703 [cs.HC]. Preprint, 2026.

    How should LLM characters take part in live multiplayer play while staying executable in the shared world, coherent with the other characters, and steerable by players? The paper organises character control around three interfaces: agent-to-agent communication kept stable with probabilistic reply-chain decay, world actions grounded through embeddings with fallbacks, and a whisper that lets players nudge a character without taking it over. Deployed in Bside: Desktop Mate; a formative analysis of the live game shows the architecture keeping interaction stable and player interventions landing.

  • Dobit to Bside: Grounding LLM Characters into Executable Gameplay

    First author. AIIDE 2026, Case Studies track. Accepted, to appear, 2026.

    A case study of three products: Bside on mobile, a character-raising game built around AI-native play; Bside: Desktop Mate, where every character is player-owned and run by a bounded multi-agent runtime; and Dobit before them, a multiplayer life simulation with an open-ended multi-agent town. What it took to turn open-ended model output into actions a game can execute, and how each step tracked what language models had become capable of and affordable enough to do. Presentation scheduled for November 2026; the paper will be linked once published.

  • Raise, Don't Replace: Two Kinds of Connection in AI Character Products

    Second author. BritCHI 2026 (39th International BCS Human-Computer Interaction Conference), Short Papers track. Accepted, to appear, 2026.

    A short paper that separates two kinds of connection an AI character product can support, connection with the character and connection between people that the character brings about, and argues for designs that raise human relationships rather than replace them. Brunel University of London, 1 to 3 November 2026.
